# WP-AF-0003 — Define the Monad Laws

## Executive Summary

Establish the concise, immutable engineering laws that summarize the Monad worldview.

The Monad Laws are not implementation requirements. They are fundamental truths that every subsystem, specification, compiler stage, and implementation should respect.

Where the Manifesto explains **why**, and the Engineering Principles explain **how**, the Monad Laws define **what must always remain true**.

---

# Problem Statement

Large engineering systems often lose conceptual integrity over time because their guiding assumptions become scattered throughout documentation and implementation.

Engineers require a concise set of architectural invariants that can be remembered, referenced, and applied consistently.

Without such invariants:

* architectural drift accelerates;
* terminology becomes inconsistent;
* implementation decisions diverge;
* specifications become internally contradictory.

Monad requires a small set of enduring engineering laws that remain stable throughout the lifetime of the project.

---

# Motivation

The Monad Laws serve as the highest-level engineering heuristics for the platform.

They should be:

* memorable;
* implementation independent;
* technology independent;
* architecture preserving;
* difficult to change.

Every future architectural discussion should remain consistent with these laws.

---

# Objectives

This work packet establishes:

1. Knowledge has primacy over representation.
2. Knowledge is captured once.
3. Knowledge is compiled before it is consumed.
4. Knowledge is never destroyed—only evolved.
5. Every fact has provenance.
6. Everything downstream is a projection.
7. The Semantic Graph is the canonical source of truth.

# Engineering Notes

The Monad Laws intentionally occupy a unique place within the architecture.

They are not specifications.

They are not implementation guidance.

They are architectural invariants.

As the Monad ecosystem evolves, specifications, implementations, and tooling may change substantially, but the Laws should remain stable unless a fundamental change in the philosophy of the project is required.
