1.

Knowledge has primacy over representation.

────────────────────────────

Knowledge exists independently of files,
languages, databases, user interfaces,
serialization formats, or programming
languages.

Representations change.

Knowledge remains.

────────────────────────────

2.

Knowledge is captured once.

────────────────────────────

Engineering knowledge should never require
manual duplication.

Every subsequent representation should be
derived from canonical knowledge.

────────────────────────────

3.

Knowledge is compiled before it is consumed.

────────────────────────────

Source documents are written by humans.

Semantic knowledge is consumed by machines.

Compilation transforms information into
understanding.

────────────────────────────

4.

Knowledge is never destroyed.

────────────────────────────

Knowledge evolves.

It is superseded.

Deprecated.

Migrated.

Withdrawn.

Archived.

History remains part of the graph.

────────────────────────────

5.

Every fact has provenance.

────────────────────────────

Every engineering conclusion must answer:

Where did this come from?

Why is it believed?

What evidence supports it?

Who introduced it?

What depends upon it?

────────────────────────────

6.

Everything downstream is a projection.

────────────────────────────

Documentation.

Websites.

Books.

Presentations.

Code.

Execution plans.

AI context.

Analytics.

Reports.

All derive from canonical knowledge.

────────────────────────────

7.

The Semantic Graph is the Source of Truth.

────────────────────────────

The compiler produces knowledge.

The Knowledge Engine preserves it.

Every other subsystem consumes it.

No downstream representation may redefine
the graph.
